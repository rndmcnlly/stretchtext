    What moves, and why

    Two layers, both inferred from the readings alone. Correspondence: each pair of neighbouring readings is split at word boundaries, punctuation apart from words, and matched word by word (ignoring case). Unique runs of two or more matched words that changed order are moves. Motion: each edit becomes a stretch envelope, which borrows neighbouring matched words until the earlier and later spans are within twice each other's length, or the whole phrase is used. An added clause is carried by the phrase it extends, never by a comma or a blank gap. The envelope's earlier words stretch to fill a growing room, pop at halfway to the later words compressed, then relax. Words outside envelopes keep their shape and glide; moved runs arc over the line. Everything on screen is always one complete reading the agent wrote.

    Limits: matching is lexical, not semantic; repeated words can match the wrong occurrence; the twofold rule counts space-separated words, not meaning, so it can borrow more or less context than a person would choose.
